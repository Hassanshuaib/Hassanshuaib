# CFO analysis: SAR 300k into a prep kitchen with B2B office coffee subscriptions (Riyadh)

Date: 2026-11-23 · Author: CFO · Currency: SAR, all prices **ex-VAT** · Stage: idea, nothing validated

## 1. Decision and financial question
Should the CEO commit SAR 300,000 to Option B (a prep kitchen in a residential district with office coffee subscriptions, delivered)? If so, how should the money be structured? The financial question is whether contribution per office can cover the fixed cost of a kitchen and team before the cash runs out, and what total funding that takes.

## 2. Inputs
**Facts / user inputs**
- Investment available: SAR 300,000 [CEO input].
- Option B chosen, Riyadh, residential district, B2B, delivery-stable products [CEO input / context.md].

**Missing inputs (these would change the conclusion)**
1. CEO's role: unpaid owner-operator or salaried/hired manager. This is the largest single swing.
2. Real kitchen rent and fit-out quote. Is there a licensable unit already food-grade?
3. Price that offices will actually pay per seat, and cups actually consumed per seat.
4. Office size (seats per account) and distance from the kitchen, which drives cost per drop.
5. Payment terms: prepaid or invoiced on 30/60 days.
6. Licensing route and cost (Balady, SFDA, Civil Defense, CR).

**Is 300k enough?**
- **Full build:** no. After opening, the 65k reserve covers about 2 months of burn, and cash goes negative around month 3–4. The **peak funding need is about SAR 677k** (300k plus a further 377k) in the Base case and about **1.34M** in the Downside, with no break-even within 36 months.
- **Lean build:** yes in the Base case. Minimum cash is +70k, so the peak draw is about 230k of the 300k. It is **not** enough in the Lean Downside, where cash bottoms at −178k and a further roughly 180k would be needed.

## 4. Unit economics per seat per month

| Line | Full Base | Lean Base | Lean Downside | Lean Upside |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | 220 | 230 | 210 | 250 |
| COGS (20 cups) | 98 | 98 | 108 | 98 |
| Payment fees | 5.5 | 5.8 | 5.3 | 6.3 |
| **Gross margin** | 116.5 (53%) | 126 (55%) | 97 (46%) | 146 (58%) |
| Delivery | 30.3 (3 drops/wk) | 20.2 (2 drops/wk) | 28.9 | 14.4 |
| **Contribution per seat** | **86 (39%)** | **106 (46%)** | **68 (32%)** | **131 (53%)** |
| Contribution per office | 1,293 (15 seats) | 1,590 | 819 (12 seats) | 2,358 (18 seats) |

Formulas: delivery per seat = drops/wk × 52/12 × cost per drop ÷ seats per office. Contribution = price − cups × COGS per cup − fee% × price − delivery per seat.
Daily delivery (5 drops/wk) cuts contribution to about SAR 66/seat in the full Base. **Delivery frequency is a product-design decision with big financial effect.** Selling a refrigerated cold-brew and batch-brew stock 2x/week is far better than daily fresh drops, and it also takes care of the 7–10am peak problem.

## 8. Cash and working capital
- Rent paid 6 months in advance creates lumpy cash outflows (48k or 30k every 6 months).
- B2B invoicing on 60 days cuts Lean Base minimum cash from +70k to +9k. **Insist on monthly prepaid subscriptions** (card or direct debit). This alone is worth about 115k of NPV versus 60-day terms.
- Inventory is small (beans, bottles). Cold brew has a short shelf life, so waste is a risk and is covered in the 10% waste allowance in A3.

## 9. Verdict
**Do not invest the full 300k now in a full-build kitchen.** In the Base case it needs about 677k in total and has a 3-year NPV of −541k. **Invest only on conditions, in stages, with a lean structure.** The model is NPV-positive (+145k) only if the CEO runs it as an unpaid owner-operator with a small, already food-grade kitchen, 2 drops a week and prepaid billing. Even then, the Lean Downside loses about 400k, so validation has to come before the lease.

**Staging**
- **Tranche 0: validation, ≤ SAR 30k, 8–12 weeks, no lease.** Produce through a licensed partner kitchen or roastery (legal check needed). Run paid pilots with offices.
  - Gate to Tranche 1: **10 signed LOIs** totalling **≥150 seats** at **≥ SAR 220/seat**, of which **≥5 offices convert to a prepaid paid pilot**. Measured cups/seat ≥ 15. Actual cost per drop ≤ SAR 35. Offices within about 5 km of the target kitchen. CEO role confirmed.
- **Tranche 1: lean build, about SAR 145k** (capex 90k, licensing 12k, rent advance 30k, launch 13k).
- **Reserve: about SAR 125k held back,** released only if, by month 6 after opening, there are **≥120 paying seats**, churn ≤ 4%/month and contribution ≥ SAR 95/seat.

**Kill criteria:** fewer than 80 paying seats at month 6 after opening; contribution below SAR 70/seat for 2 consecutive months; cash below SAR 40k without a funded plan; or fewer than 10 LOIs at the end of Tranche 0, which means do not sign a lease.

**Confidence: Medium** that the full build is unsound, since that holds across most of the range. **Low** on the Lean Base being profitable, because every driver is an estimate. The answer would change with real LOI pricing and seat counts, a firm rent and fit-out quote, and the CEO's salary needs.

## 10. Reproducibility
The model is a monthly 36-month simulation. Offices(t) = offices(t−1) × (1 − churn) + adds, starting after 2 pre-opening months. Revenue = seats × price. Variable cost = seats × (COGS + fees + delivery per seat). Fixed cost = rent + staff (+6k step) + lead + other. Cash collections lag revenue by DSO months. Rent is paid 6 months in advance. Cash flow at month 0 is the upfront spend. The monthly discount rate is (1.20)^(1/12) − 1. IRR is solved by bisection on the monthly flows and annualised. Python scripts (`model.py`, `lean.py`) were run in the CFO session scratchpad and are not stored in the project. All figures above come from those runs.
