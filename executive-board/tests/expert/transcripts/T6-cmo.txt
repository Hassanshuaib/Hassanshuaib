**Who was consulted:** the CMO (`executive-board:cmo`). All inputs are your fictional figures. I re-checked the main numbers by hand and they match. The CMO's "Checks:" line says it used the board calculator and cross-checked with a simulation.

**(a) Checkout A/B test**
- Conversion is 4.10% for control and 4.61% for the variant, a +12.5% relative lift.
- z = 1.27 and **p = 0.20**, so it is not significant.
- The 95% range for the lift runs from **−6% to +35%**. The variant could actually be worse.
- The test only had about **25% power**. A test that weak tends to overstate the lift it finds.
- Detecting a 10% relative lift (α 0.05, power 0.8) needs **about 38,500 visitors per arm**, roughly 77k in total. You have about 5k per arm now.

These are healthy numbers, but not a lot of headroom if churn rises.

**(c) Price increase, 79 → 89**
- **MRR stays the same if you lose up to 11.2% of subscribers straight away.**
- **Steady-state gross profit stays the same if monthly churn rises to:**
  - **5.38%** if cost per subscriber stays fixed at 27.65 SAR. The CMO recommends this view, since coffee costs don't rise with the price.
  - **5.07%** if margin is held at 65% of price. The CMO suggests treating this as the stricter limit until the CFO confirms costs.
- **This assumes the same number of new subscribers per month at 89.** That is optimistic: a higher price on the checkout page will probably lower conversion. This is the biggest risk in the price case.
- **If churn doesn't change at 89:** LTV:CAC rises to 3.6x and payback drops to 5.1 months.

**Recommendation**
1. **Checkout variant:** Confidence: High.
   - Keep the test running to about 38.5k visitors per arm.
   - Fix the stopping rule in advance and don't check results early.
   - Measure paid conversion, and watch refunds and first-month churn alongside it.
2. **Price 89:** Confidence: Medium.
   - Run 79 vs 89 on new signups only, and judge it on gross profit per visitor.
   - Keep it separate from the checkout test so the two effects don't mix.
   - Limits: conversion should fall by less than about 16% relative, and churn in months 1–2 should stay below about 5.1–5.4%. The 16% limit assumes churn doesn't change.
   - Only then move existing subscribers. Give them 2–3 months' notice at the old price and start with 10–20% of them.

The confidence would drop if cost per subscriber turns out to rise with price, or if conversion or churn at 89 come in worse than those limits.
