**Reconciling the two methods**
- **Size of the gap:** the exit case gives an EV 18.1% higher than Gordon. At equity level the gap widens to 22.8%, because the SAR 20m of net debt is subtracted from both.
- **Why the exit case is harder to defend:** 4.44% growth forever would need a return on new investment of about 23.6% indefinitely, which is unrealistic for an asset-heavy logistics business. At 3% growth the required return is about 15.9%.
- **What closes the gap:** either an exit multiple of 7.2x, or Gordon growth of 4.44%. Cutting WACC by 1 point at 3% growth also gets close (9.33).

**Biggest risk:** the terminal value is 72–77% of EV, so the valuation rests mostly on the terminal assumption, not the five-year forecast.

**Assumptions the CFO added:**
- Net debt is at market value, with no other debt-like items.
- Tax is a flat 20% on EBIT. Zakat versus corporate income tax in Saudi Arabia needs a tax adviser.
- Year 6 reinvestment is carried at Year 5's rate, which makes the Gordon value slightly conservative.
