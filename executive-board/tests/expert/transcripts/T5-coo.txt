At 12 stations, capacity exactly equals demand, so any random swing creates a backlog that lasts the whole peak.

**(b) Cups** (service level 95%, z = 1.645)

- **Reorder point:** 8,261 cups, about 6.9 days of stock.
- **Order size (EOQ):** about 56,900 cups. That is 7.6 orders a year, one every 47 days.
- **Annual cost:** about SAR 6,831, split equally between ordering and holding.

**(c) Oven**
- **OEE:** 75.0%, from availability 90.0%, performance 88.0% and quality 94.7%.
- **Biggest loss:** the oven runs slower than its ideal speed, which costs about 65 minutes. Downtime costs 60 minutes and the 50 rejected units cost about 25.

**Where 14 stations could fail:** 14 has very little margin.
- If orders arrive 10% faster, or packing is 10% slower, you need 15.
- If both happen, you need 17.
- If one packer is absent, you drop to 13, which fails the target.
- The model may overstate waits because real packing times are fairly steady. It may understate them because delivery-app orders arrive in bursts.

**What I recommend:**
1. **Packing:** run 14 stations at the peak, keep a 15th on standby and cross-train staff to cover it. Before you buy any equipment, measure real order arrivals and packing times in 15-minute slots for two weeks.
2. **Cups:** reorder at 8,261 and order about 57,000 at a time, rounded to the supplier's pack size. Negotiate a more reliable delivery time with the supplier. Halving how much it varies would cut the safety stock by about 35%.
3. **Oven:** fix the slow running first, then downtime and rejects. Aim for 80% OEE within 90 days.

Confidence: medium-high. Measured arrival and packing data could raise the number of stations needed to 15 or more.
