**Why A wins**
1. **NPV and IRR agree.** Normally the bigger project wins on NPV while the smaller one wins on IRR, and NPV should decide. That doesn't happen here. A's extra 1.3M of capex over B earns about 34%, well above the 11% hurdle, and adds about 1.68M of value.
2. **The different lives don't change the answer.** The projects are mutually exclusive, last 6 and 4 years, and can be repeated. So the right comparison is EAA, or NPV over a 12-year repeat cycle. On both, A delivers about ten times B's value per year, and the two methods agree.
3. **A also wins if capital is limited:** its PI of 1.75 means it creates more value per riyal invested.
4. **Payback measures speed, not value.** B is ahead only because it is smaller and A has a large working-capital build in year 1. Payback matters only if cash is tight in the next 3–4 years.

**Sensitivities**
- **Working-capital timing:** if the year-1 working capital is invested at t0 instead, A's NPV falls to 1.748M. A still wins.
- **Discount rate:** the ranking flips only at about 35.9%.
- **Downside case** (price −5%, variable cost 60%, volume −10%): A's NPV is 660K, still well above B.
- **Break-even, one driver at a time:** A falls to B's level only if the year-1 price drops to 69.6 (−27%) or variable cost rises to 68.9% of revenue. If the downside volume and cost happen together, the break-even price is about 81.
